## Problem statement: one line

Young people moving to Indian cities choose PGs blind, get roommates by chance, and live in places run on notebooks and WhatsApp, which leads to early move-outs, wasted food and deposit fights for residents and independent owners alike.

## Problem statement: summary (825 characters)

Every year, students and young professionals move to cities like Chennai and into paying-guest (PG) homes. They pick one from scattered listings and WhatsApp forwards, often without knowing the real monthly cost. Once there, they are given a bed next to whoever is already in the room, so differences in sleep, noise and tidiness turn into conflict and early move-outs. Daily life (rent, food, complaints, deposits) is tracked in notebooks and chats, so service slips and move-outs end in disputes over the deposit. Most PGs are run by independent owners who have no system built for them: management apps only collect rent, listing portals stop at move-in, and branded co-living serves only its own buildings. The result is a stressful start for residents, empty beds and wasted food for owners, and low trust on both sides.

## How Living Plus solves it (if the form asks)

Living Plus is one platform for the guest, the resident and the owner. Guests see the real monthly cost and live beds, then talk to VibeCheck, a voice PG buddy in Tamil, Hindi or English that matches them with roommates who live like them. Residents get one app for rent, issues, meals and notices. Owners get an AI copilot that drafts the routine work for their approval, a kitchen forecast that cuts food waste, and Deposit Shield, which settles move-outs fairly from move-in and move-out photos.
