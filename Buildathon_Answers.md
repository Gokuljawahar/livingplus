## Q1. Provide one sentence summary of your new product/project that you are planning to build. *(text, max 1024 chars)*

LivingPlus is a planned experience platform for urban migration that would guide students and young professionals through the whole journey of moving to a new city — from arrival, verified stays near work or study, compatible roommates, food and cleaning, and transparent payments, to a fair and documented move-out — while giving independent apartment and paying-guest (PG) owners a simple modular console to run their properties and switch on only the services they need, with AI assistants supporting residents, owners and staff throughout.

## Q2. What is the main problem that you are solving with the new product idea? *(text, max 1024 chars)*

Moving to a new city for study or work is stressful, slow and untrustworthy. Discovery is adversarial: listings are often fake or misleading, broker fees are high, and advance-payment fraud is common, so newcomers can search for weeks and still choose badly. Daily living is unmanaged: food, cleaning, rent receipts, complaints, dues and deposits are handled through notebooks and chat messages, so service quality drops after move-in and disputes are common at move-out. Supply is fragmented: most urban rentals are run by small independent owners with no common tools, while branded operators serve only their own properties. The result is a poor experience for residents, low trust on both sides, and owners competing on rent cuts instead of service quality — while the everyday services around every stay remain unorganised and uncaptured.

## Q3. Who would be a typical user/customer? *(text, max 1024 chars)*

The primary users would be young people moving to a new city — students, coaching aspirants and early-career professionals, roughly 18–30 — along with parents who often help fund and vet the stay. The paying customers would be independent apartment and PG owners, from single-property owners to small multi-property operators, who want better occupancy, calmer day-to-day operations and additional income from services. Day-to-day operational users would include wardens, cooks, housekeeping staff and accountants, each with limited, role-based access. Local food, cleaning and mobility providers would participate as service partners, gaining steady demand concentrated in each property. The commercial logic is simple: residents and staff use the platform without paying, owners pay for the tools and visibility, and partners share revenue — so growth focuses on signing up owners while resident numbers per property make the partner services worthwhile.

## Q4. What is your solution to this problem? *(text, max 1024 chars)*

LivingPlus is planned as one platform with three parts. First, a resident experience covering the full journey: verified listings near work or college, help finding compatible roommates, secure booking, guided move-in, flexible food options, on-demand cleaning, digital payments with receipts, easy complaint reporting, clear dues, and a fair, documented move-out. Second, a modular console for owners: each property gets its own private workspace where the owner chooses which services to offer — such as food plans, arrival pickup, cleaning or messaging integrations — and gives staff only the access their role needs, plus simple analytics. Third, AI assistance woven through both sides: helpers for arrival, daily living and move-out on the resident side, and operations, pricing and staff coordination on the owner side, with human approval for anything involving money or records. The core idea is to give every small property the experience quality of a large branded operator, without forcing it to become one.

## Q6. What is the potential business model of this product? *(text, max 1024 chars)*

The potential model has three complementary revenue streams. First, a subscription for owners, priced per property or per bed, with optional paid add-on modules — for example food management, service integrations or advanced AI assistance — so owners pay only for what they switch on and revenue grows as they adopt more. Second, transaction income, such as a commission on confirmed bookings and optional paid promotion for verified listings that helps owners fill vacancies faster. Third, a revenue share from everyday services ordered through the platform — food, cleaning and local transport — where concentrated resident demand in each property makes the services attractive to partners. Residents and staff would not be charged, which keeps adoption high on the demand side; more residents per property makes partner services more valuable, and that value helps keep owner pricing accessible. Over time, owners running several properties on one system would find it natural to standardise on it, supporting retention.

## Q7. Who will be your primary competitor(s) and how is your solution different from them? *(text, max 1024 chars)*

Competition falls into three groups. Branded co-living operators (such as Stanza Living, Zolo and Colive) offer a managed living experience, but only inside their own leased or managed property networks, so independent owners get no benefit. Listing marketplaces (such as NoBroker, MagicBricks and 99acres) help people find rooms and some add agreements or rent collection, but they largely step away once the stay begins, leaving food, cleaning, support and move-out unmanaged. Legacy management software is accounting-focused and rigid for small owners. LivingPlus is designed to be different on two counts: it would cover the full journey from arrival to move-out, and it would work on any independent owner's property rather than only its own network, with modular services each property can switch on or off. If adopted, familiarity with each property's configuration, local partner relationships and everyday workflows could become lasting advantages, plus verified listings and two-way reviews.

## Q8. What will be your primary and secondary target countries, if applicable? *(text, max 1024 chars)*

The primary market is India, which has one of the world's largest flows of people moving cities for education and work, a large and growing paying-guest and rental market, and a supply base dominated by small independent owners — exactly the fragmentation LivingPlus is designed to organise. The initial focus would be major education and employment hubs, growing through clusters of owners near campuses and office corridors. Secondary markets, longer term, would be countries with similarly dense renting and fragmented small-owner supply, such as the UAE and parts of Southeast Asia (for example the Philippines and Indonesia), where many residents are migrants and shared housing is common. The same core product — resident journey, modular owner console, AI assistance — would carry across, with localisation limited mainly to language, payments, regulations and service partners, so expansion is planned as adaptation rather than a rebuild.

## Q10. How are you planning to use AI for this solution? *(text, max 1024 chars)*

AI is planned as the operating layer of LivingPlus, not a bolt-on feature. For residents, AI assistants would help at each step: planning arrival and move-in, matching compatible roommates from habits and preferences, answering everyday questions, explaining dues in plain language, accepting complaints by photo or voice, helping order food or cleaning, sending gentle reminders before payments are due, and guiding a fair move-out with clear deduction explanations. For owners, an AI co-pilot would summarise occupancy, dues, complaints and upcoming renewals, suggest actions, draft routine notices, help price vacant rooms, follow up with enquiries, and coordinate staff tasks with a clear record of what was done. Safety is part of the design from the start: each property's data stays separate, every user sees only what their role allows, and anything involving money or official records requires human approval. The goal is to turn support from a cost burden into the product's main strength.

## Q12. Please describe how are you planning to leverage the above selected Google's AI models to tackle the problem you mentioned? *(text, max 1024 chars)*

We plan to use the Gemini API as the reasoning backbone for the platform's AI assistants — helping residents and owners across arrival, roommate matching, daily living, move-out, operations and staff coordination — connected to product functions such as search, bookings, dues, complaints and tasks, with strict per-property and per-role limits on what each assistant can see or do. Prompts and behaviour would be developed and tested in AI Studio against role-based test cases (for example, a resident must never see another resident's dues, and a warden must never see finances) before any release. Gemini Live would provide the voice-first, local-language interface: many first-time migrants are far more comfortable speaking than typing, so complaints, questions and owner briefings should work hands-free by voice. This gives one consistent intelligence with two interfaces — precise, tool-based workflows where accuracy matters, and natural voice interaction where accessibility matters — under the same safety rules.

## Q14. How do you intend to deploy and scale your product on Google Cloud? *(text, max 1024 chars)*

The plan is to build on managed Google Cloud services so the product can start small and scale without re-architecture. Firebase Hosting would serve the web experience quickly worldwide through its content delivery network. Cloud Run would host the application services, scaling automatically with demand — including seasonal peaks around admissions and hiring — and scaling down when idle to control cost. Cloud SQL (managed PostgreSQL) would hold the multi-tenant product data with automated backups, keeping every property's data logically separate. Cloud Storage would hold property photos and verification documents with controlled, time-limited access. Google Maps would power location-based discovery near campuses and workplaces, and Cloud Logging and Monitoring would provide reliability tracking and alerts. New capabilities would roll out gradually per property through configuration, so growth comes from onboarding more properties, not from rebuilding the system.
